# Three-Position Synthesis

Three-position synthesis is used to design a four-bar linkage that moves a coupler through three specified positions. There are two common cases: **Finding Fixed Pivots** and **With Fixed Pivots**.

## Finding Fixed Pivots

In this case, you determine the fixed pivot locations based on the three coupler positions.

1. Take a screenshot from the [reference video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g6TFqsvxqC4) to define the three coupler positions
2. Draw a **Segment** with points **P1** and **P2** on top of the first coupler position
3. Use **Compass** with P1 and P2 as radius
4. Place the compass center at the first point of the second position (**P3**)
5. Draw a **Segment** from P3 to **P4**, where P4 is on the compass circle and aligned with the second position
6. (Optional) Hide the compass circle
7. Use **Compass** again with P1 and P2 as radius
8. Place the compass center at the first point of the third position (**P5**)
9. Draw a **Segment** from P5 to **P6**, where P6 is on the compass circle and aligned with the third position
10. (Optional) Hide the compass circle

11. Draw a **Segment** between P1 and P3
12. Draw a **Segment** between P3 and P5
13. Construct the **Perpendicular Bisector** of segment P1-P3
14. Construct the **Perpendicular Bisector** of segment P3-P5
15. Place a point **P7** at the intersection of the two bisectors as the first fixed pivot

16. Repeat steps 11-15 for P2, P4, and P6 to find the second fixed pivot (**P8**)

:::info Discussion
The above example yields a **double-rocker** linkage with **limited angular motion** bounded by **toggle positions**. The mechanism cannot fully reach the third position due to its **kinematic limits** — a key distinction between synthesis intent and feasible motion in **non-Grashof mechanisms**.

**Actuation choice** also matters: driving the other rocker causes the coupler to follow a different **motion branch**, failing to reach positions 2 and 3 due to **kinematic singularities**. This **input-output asymmetry** means driving different rockers is not equivalent.

For limited-motion mechanisms, **reachability** and **actuation** must be evaluated after synthesis, especially near **toggle configurations**.
:::

## With Fixed Pivots

In this case, you are given the fixed pivot locations and need to find the moving pivots on the coupler.

1. Take a screenshot from the [reference video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g6TFqsvxqC4) to define the three coupler positions and fixed pivots
2. Draw a **Segment** with points **P1** and **P2** on top of the first coupler position
3. Use **Compass** with P1 and P2 as radius
4. Place the compass center at the first point of the second position (**P3**)
5. Draw a **Segment** from P3 to **P4**, where P4 is on the compass circle and aligned with the second position
6. (Optional) Hide the compass circle
7. Use **Compass** again with P1 and P2 as radius
8. Place the compass center at the first point of the third position (**P5**)
9. Draw a **Segment** from P5 to **P6**, where P6 is on the compass circle and aligned with the third position
10. (Optional) Hide the compass circle
11. Draw a **Segment** with points **P7** and **P8** on top of the given fixed pivots

12. Use **Compass** with P3 and P7 as radius, P1 as center
13. Use **Compass** with P4 and P7 as radius, P2 as center
14. Place a point **P9** at the intersection of the two compass circles
15. Use **Compass** with P3 and P8 as radius, P1 as center
16. Use **Compass** with P4 and P8 as radius, P2 as center
17. Place a point **P10** at the intersection of the two compass circles
18. (Recommended) Hide the compass circles
19. Draw a **Segment** between P9 and P10

:::tip
The pattern of creating two compass circles and finding their intersection is used to **copy a triangle** when one side has the same length. For example, triangle P3-P4-P7 is copied to P1-P2-P9.
:::

20. Use **Compass** with P5 and P7 as radius, P1 as center
21. Use **Compass** with P6 and P7 as radius, P2 as center
22. Place a point **P11** at the intersection of the two compass circles
23. Use **Compass** with P5 and P8 as radius, P1 as center
24. Use **Compass** with P6 and P8 as radius, P2 as center
25. Place a point **P12** at the intersection of the two compass circles
26. (Recommended) Hide the compass circles
27. Draw a **Segment** between P11 and P12

  src={
    '/video/geometry-constructions-for-mechanism-design/three-position-synthesis-with-fixed-pivots-3.mp4'

28. Draw a **Segment** between P7 and P9
29. Draw a **Segment** between P9 and P11
30. Construct the **Perpendicular Bisector** of each segment
31. Place a point **P13** at the intersection of the two bisectors as the first moving pivot
32. Repeat steps 28-31 for P8, P10, and P12 to find the second moving pivot (**P14**)

<DocAnimation
  src={
    '/video/geometry-constructions-for-mechanism-design/three-position-synthesis-with-fixed-pivots-4.mp4'
  }

  border={true}
/>

33. Temporarily treat **P13** and **P14** as fixed pivots and **P7** and **P8** as moving pivots
34. Sketch a four-bar linkage
35. Add an actuator to the joint at P14
36. Verify the four-bar passes through segments P7-P8, P9-P10, and P11-P12

<DocAnimation
  src={
    '/video/geometry-constructions-for-mechanism-design/three-position-synthesis-with-fixed-pivots-5.mp4'
  }

  border={true}
/>

37. Remove the actuator
38. Invert the four-bar linkage by making the link P7-P8 the new ground
39. This inverts P13-P14 back to moving pivots and P7-P8 to fixed pivots
40. Extend the coupler (now the link P13-P14) to include the original segment points P1 and P2
41. Add an actuator at the joint of point P8
